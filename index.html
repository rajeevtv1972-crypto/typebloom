Typing practice guide

Build typing speed with accuracy first

Typing is a practical skill used in school, work, programming, communication, and everyday life. A useful practice session gives you a clear task, feedback you can understand, and one small point to improve next time. TypeBloom combines short progressive passages with longer timed sessions so you can practise a specific skill and then measure how it carries over.

How WPM and CPM are calculated

Words per minute (WPM) uses a five-character standard for a word. TypeBloom calculates it by dividing correctly matched characters by five, then dividing by elapsed minutes. Characters per minute (CPM) uses the same correct-character count without the five-character conversion. For example, 250 correct characters in one minute is 50 WPM or 250 CPM. These are useful estimates of speed, but they do not describe every part of writing quality.

Read speed together with accuracy

Accuracy is the percentage of entered characters that match their target positions. A faster result is not automatically a better result if it includes many mismatches. Try to establish a comfortable pace first, then increase speed in small steps while keeping your accuracy steady. Comparing several similar sessions is more informative than judging your ability from a single unusually fast or slow attempt.

A ten-minute practice routine

  1. Warm up for two minutes. Choose a comfortable passage and focus on relaxed hands and consistent finger placement.
  2. Practise accuracy for three minutes. Slow down around the letters, spaces, or punctuation that often cause mistakes.
  3. Build flow for three minutes. Read a little ahead, keep your shoulders relaxed, and avoid rushing to catch up.
  4. Review for two minutes. Check your speed, accuracy, and common mistakes. Choose one pattern to work on next time.

Use mistakes as useful feedback

When the same key or letter pair causes trouble more than once, isolate that pattern rather than repeating a full test without a plan. TypeBloom's weak-spot workout uses the most common mismatches from a completed challenge to build a short follow-up passage. Treat it as a drill: practise slowly, notice the movement, and return to a normal challenge when the pattern feels more familiar.

Choose the right kind of session

Progressive levels help you build from shorter passages to more varied text. The Daily Challenge offers a one-minute session with a 95% accuracy target. Speed tests focus on WPM, while CPM tests focus on correctly matched characters. Longer tests can help you see whether your rhythm stays consistent after the first few minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an account?

No. The core exercises work in the browser. TypeBloom saves session summaries, completed levels, and preferences in local browser storage so you can return to your progress on the same browser.

Why do my scores change between sessions?

Passage length, punctuation, numbers, familiarity with the text, and concentration all affect a result. Compare similar test types over several sessions to get a clearer picture of your progress.

Where can I learn more?

Read the TypeBloom typing guide for a detailed explanation of WPM, CPM, accuracy, touch-typing technique, practice planning, and how to review common errors.
